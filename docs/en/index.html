Three things it does

1. Touch correction — the pointer lands where your finger did

Turn the panel 90° and macOS still receives its coordinates in the old orientation. That is why the pointer jumps sideways, or runs the opposite way to your finger. TouchRotator reads the panel's own USB signal and corrects the coordinates there, so your trackpad and mouse behave exactly as before.

Settings → Touch: choose the display to correct and run detect by tapping once. After that, ⌥⌘R turns it on and off.

2. Display settings — one click, without opening System Settings

Resolution, brightness, mirroring and rotation, from the menu bar popup. One card per display, as many as you have plugged in.

Click the menu bar icon → press a display's name to open its card.

3. Window layout — plug the monitor back in, get your desk back

Unplug a monitor and the windows that were on it are herded onto the built-in display; plugging it back in does not bring them home. TouchRotator remembers where they were and puts them back at the same position and size.

Arrange things how you like them, then press Remember this layout once. From then on it happens by itself, and Restore layout brings it back any time.
